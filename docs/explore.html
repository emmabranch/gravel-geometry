Explore the bikes

Stack and reach for every size of 24 current gravel bikes, with filters and the key handling numbers.

Every dot is one frame size. Lines join the sizes of the same bike, smallest to largest. Up means a taller front end. Right means a longer one.

  • Stack is how high the top of the head tube sits above the pedals’ axle (the bottom bracket).
  • Reach is how far forward it sits.
  • Stack-to-reach ratio sums up the posture. Race bikes sit lower and longer (around 1.40), adventure bikes taller and shorter (1.50 and up). The faint diagonal lines mark ratios so you can see the clusters.
// Shared chart settings from the brand kit (Space Mono axes and defaults)
import {ebPlot} from "./js/eb_charts.js"
raw = FileAttachment("data/gravel_geometry_derived.csv").csv({typed: true})
import {cockpit, partsFor, DEFAULTS} from "./js/rider.js"

// R writes TRUE/FALSE; make them true booleans
bikes = raw.map(d => ({
  ...d,
  is_default_config: d.is_default_config === true || d.is_default_config === "TRUE",
  offset_estimated: d.offset_estimated === true || d.offset_estimated === "TRUE",
  size_label: String(d.size_label),
  label: `${d.brand} ${d.model}`
})).map(d => {
  // Handlebar position as sold: stock stem (or the standard one for a 176 cm
  // rider where the brand doesn't say) on 20 mm of spacers
  const parts = partsFor(d, 1760);
  const bar = cockpit(d, {...DEFAULTS, ...parts.settings}).bar;
  const stemKnown = !parts.assumed.includes("stem length");
  return {...d, bar_reach: bar.x, bar_stack: bar.y, stem_known: stemKnown,
    stem_desc: `${parts.settings.stemLength} mm at ${parts.settings.stemAngle}°${stemKnown ? (parts.assumed.includes("stem angle") ? ", angle not stated" : "") : ", not stated"}`};
})

All lengths are in millimetres and angles in degrees. Trail, toe clearance, and estimated fork offsets are calculated by this site, not published by the manufacturers.

What the columns mean

Stack/reach
Stack divided by reach. Higher means a more upright riding position.
Bar stack and bar reach
Where the handlebar sits as sold: the vertical and horizontal distance from the bottom bracket to the centre of the stem’s bar clamp, with each size’s stock stem on 20 mm of spacers. Where a brand doesn’t say which stem it fits, a standard stem for a 176 cm rider is assumed (90 mm at −6°). Frame stack and reach compare frames; bar stack and reach compare where your hands go out of the box.
Head angle
How steeply the steering axis is tilted. Slacker (lower) angles feel calmer at speed. Steeper (higher) angles feel quicker to turn.
Seat angle
How far forward the saddle sits over the pedals. Steeper angles put you further forward.
Fork offset
How far the fork pushes the front axle ahead of the steering axis. Three brands don’t publish it, so it’s worked back from the wheelbase. Checked against bikes that do publish it, that method lands within 2 mm for 115 of 118 sizes.
Trail
The gap on the ground between where the steering axis points and where the tyre touches down, calculated for a 700 × 45 mm tyre. More trail means steadier, self-centring steering. Gravel bikes mostly sit between 60 and 80 mm.
Wheelbase
The distance between the axles. Longer is steadier, shorter is nimbler.
Chainstay
The distance from the pedals’ axle to the rear axle. Shorter feels livelier. Longer is steadier and leaves more room for tyres.
BB drop
How far the pedals’ axle sits below the wheel axles. More drop puts you lower between the wheels, which feels planted.
Toe clearance
The gap between the pedal axle (crank pointing forward, 172.5 mm cranks) and the front tyre. A shoe usually reaches 90 to 110 mm ahead of the pedal axle, so smaller figures suggest your toe could touch the tyre in tight, slow turns.
Tyre clearance
The widest tyre the manufacturer says fits, in their own words in the data file.
